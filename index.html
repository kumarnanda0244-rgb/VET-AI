Next-Gen Agritech

Collar-Free Automated Cattle Monitoring

AI camera systems for dairy and beef cattle use computer vision, machine learning, and thermal or RGB imaging to monitor health and welfare parameters automatically without physical collars or transponders.

1. Live Camera & Upload

Inspect cattle using a live webcam / smartphone camera stream or upload high-resolution photographs for instant computer vision processing.

2. RGB & Thermal Vision

Dual-spectrum imaging identifies localized inflammation, superficial muzzle temperature, and early subclinical mastitis heat signatures.

3. Disease Detection

Automated pattern detection for Lumpy Skin Disease (nodules), FMD (mucosal drool), Mastitis, Bovine Respiratory Disease, and Pinkeye.

4. Emergency Priority Alerts

Color-coded 4-tier triage (Green, Yellow, Orange, Red) automatically flags acute complications like respiratory collapse for rapid escalation.

5. Veterinary Assistance

Connect directly with verified local veterinary dispensaries and mobile clinics. Transmit structured AI camera findings with a single tap.

6. Community Disease Intelligence

Anonymized symptom reports and camera findings are aggregated at the village level to detect early outbreak clusters before widespread herd mortality occurs.

Main Innovation

Beyond Individual Monitoring.
Towards Community Protection.

Individual treatment comes too late when contagious diseases sweep through a village. VETAI’s Village-Level Disease Early Warning System detects hidden patterns before an outbreak can devastate local livelihoods.

Early Warning Detection Pipeline:

Farmer Reports → Community Analysis → Pattern Detection → Possible Cluster Warning → Veterinary Investigation
Complete Anonymity: Farmer names and phone numbers are never shared in community alerts; only village name and clinical indicators.
Intelligent Symptom Clustering: Detects multiple concurrent cases of blister lesions, acute respiratory distress, or severe diarrheal scours.
Proactive Quarantine & Ring Vaccination: Enables veterinary officers to intervene within 24 hours of the first micro-cluster.
